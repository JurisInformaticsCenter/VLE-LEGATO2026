About the VLE-LEGATO Workshop

The Workshop on Verifying Law and Ethics, and Legal Autoformalisation (VLE-LEGATO) will be held at JURIX 2026 on December 8th 2026, in Toulouse, France. It aims at bringing together research on the lifecycle of normative AI through two complementary tracks: LEGATO, focusing on norm extraction, autoformalisation, and validation; and VLE, focusing on norm integration, compliance verification, auditing, and certification. Together, VLE-LEGATO connects faithful normative representations with AI systems that comply with legal, ethical, regulatory, and other normative requirements.

Aims and Scope

AI systems are increasingly deployed in contexts where their behaviour should be constrained not only by technical requirements but also by laws, regulations, standards, ethical principles, organisational policies, and social norms. Ensuring that such systems behave in accordance with these norms raises fundamental challenges across the entire AI lifecycle. The workshop addresses three interconnected questions: (1) How can norms be extracted, formalised, and represented faithfully? (2) How can they be incorporated into AI systems’ reasoning and decision-making? (3) How can compliance with them be verified, audited, and certified?

Recent advances in NLP and LLMs create new opportunities for automatically transforming normative texts into formal representations. However, a syntactically valid representation may omit relevant conditions, misrepresent exceptions, or introduce unintended interpretations. Establishing the faithfulness of formalised norms is therefore an essential foundation for assessing the compliance of systems that use them. Integrating these representations into AI systems introduces further challenges concerning normative reasoning, conflicts between requirements, system behaviour, and human oversight.

VLE-LEGATO will address these complementary challenges through two tracks:

  • LEGATO Track: Autoformalisation and Validation of Norms. This track focuses on extracting norms from natural-language and other sources, generating formal representations, and assessing whether those representations faithfully capture the relevant normative meaning and inferential behaviour. It also addresses benchmarking, debugging, human feedback, and the maintenance of normative knowledge, with particular attention to legal autoformalisation.

  • VLE Track: Verification of Legal and Ethical Compliance. This track focuses on incorporating norms into AI architectures, reasoning, and decision-making, and on verifying, auditing, and certifying system compliance. It also addresses conflicts between legal and ethical requirements, compliance monitoring, and governance.

Contributions spanning both tracks are particularly welcome, including methods that connect the validation of normative representations with the verification of system behaviour.

Workshop Tracks

LEGATO — Autoformalisation and Validation of Norms

Extracting norms from natural-language sources, generating formal representations and validating their faithfulness, including benchmarking, debugging, human feedback and normative knowledge maintenance.

VLE — Verification of Legal and Ethical Compliance

Integrating norms into AI reasoning and decision-making, verifying, auditing and certifying compliance, and addressing normative conflicts, monitoring and governance.

Contributions bridging both tracks are particularly welcome.

Topics of Interest

LEGATO Track: Autoformalisation and Validation of Norms

  • Automatic and semi-automatic extraction, formalisation, and representation of norms from statutes, regulations, contracts, judgments, policies, and other sources
  • LLM-based, neuro-symbolic, rule-based, and ontology-based approaches to normative representation
  • Validation of source faithfulness, including conditions, exceptions, cross-references, and temporal scope
  • Semantic and behavioural equivalence of alternative normative representations
  • Testing, counterexample generation, and debugging of generated formal representations
  • Ambiguity, uncertainty, implicit assumptions, and alternative normative interpretations
  • Human-in-the-loop workflows, interfaces, and expert feedback
  • Datasets, shared tasks, benchmarks, evaluation metrics, and reproducibility
  • Traceability, versioning, maintenance, and governance of normative knowledge

VLE Track: Verification of Legal and Ethical Compliance

  • Normative reasoning, including obligations, permissions, prohibitions, exceptions, and conflicts
  • Integration of legal and ethical norms into AI decision-making, world models, and autonomous systems
  • Symbolic, machine-learning, LLM-based, and hybrid architectures incorporating normative constraints
  • Specification and verification of ethical properties, including fairness, transparency, explainability, and non-discrimination
  • Representation and management of conflicts between legal requirements and ethical considerations
  • Formal verification, testing, counterexample generation, and debugging of AI systems subject to normative requirements
  • Algorithmic auditing and compliance monitoring
  • Certification, standardisation, and labelling of intelligent systems
  • Practical applications, governance, regulatory frameworks, and case studies

Joint Topics

  • End-to-end methods connecting norm extraction and autoformalisation with system compliance verification
  • Propagation of uncertainty and interpretive choices from normative sources to system behaviour
  • Traceability from source norms through formal representations to compliance evidence
  • Shared benchmarks evaluating both representation faithfulness and system compliance
  • Human oversight throughout the normative AI lifecycle

Submissions

Contribution submissions should follow the JURIX format, i.e. all submissions should be formatted using the styles and guidelines in the IOS Press Instructions for Authors https://www.iospress.com/book-article-instructions and prepared for single-blind peer review. Submissions must comply with Sage’s ethics policies, including its artificial intelligence policy https://www.sagepub.com/journals/publication-ethics-policies/artificial-intelligence-policy.

VLE-LEGATO workshop welcomes two categories of contributions: long (10 pages) and short (5 pages). Descriptions of preliminary results or innovative ideas are strongly encouraged. The papers, together with a summary of the discussion, will be submitted for publication in the CEUR-WS.org proceedings.

Authors should submit their contribution on the track they would like to present their work:

  • Contributions for the VLE track should be submitted on: https://easychair.org/conferences/?conf=vle26
